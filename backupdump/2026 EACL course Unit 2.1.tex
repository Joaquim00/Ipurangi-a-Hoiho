\section{Suprafixes}

\subsection{Suprafixes 1}
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
Suprafix: suprasegmental affix (e.g. tone, stress)\\
Tonal affixes in the case of Cantonese\\
\medskip
\textbf{tonal affix ≠ tone-sandhi}\quad {\footnotesize although both are called 變調 in Chinese}\\
\underline{Tone-sandhi}:\\
\textsuperscript{(largely)} automatic \& predictable process of tones changing, \\
\quad given a particular phonological / syntactic environment\\
\medskip
e.g. Standard Mandarin tone 3 sandhi rule\\
買馬 mǎi mǎ > mái mǎ (= 埋馬 mái mǎ)\\
\smallskip
e.g. Shanghainese\\
三杯 se˥˧ pe˥˧ > se˥ pe˨˩\\
四杯 sz˧˦ pe˥˧ > sz˧ pe˦\\ 

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
Tone Sandhi in Cantonese?\\
e.g. Cheung 張 (1969)\\ 
differing sandhi rules of ˥˧ > ˥ in Canton vs Hong Kong\\
\medskip
\emph{However, it is very hard to find people nowadays who contrast ˥˧ and ˥ consistently\\
\quad Hong Kong is nowadays basically all ˥}\\
\smallskip
Most Cantonese speakers have no tone sandhi

\vfill \setstretch{0.65} {\tiny Cheung, Yat-Shing 張日昇. 1969. 香港粵語陰平調及變調問題 A study on the upper
even tone and changed tones in Cantonese as spoken in Hong Kong. \emph{The Journal of The Institute of Chinese Studies of The Chinese
University of Hong Kong} 香港中文大學中國文化研究所學報 2: 81–107. (\url{https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/journal/articles/v02p081.pdf}).} 

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
\textbf{\underline{Grammatical suprafix}} {\scriptsize (or complex affix with a floating tone)}:\\
{\small \emph{(Suprafixes in Standard Cantonese are tone-substitutive)}}\\
\medskip
e.g. verbal:\\
\medskip
食 sik6 \enquote*{eat} > sik6*2 (= sik6 zo2 食咗  eat \textsc{pfv})\\
\smallskip
nei5 sik6*2 faan6 mei6 aa3?\\
\textsc{2sg} eat:\textsc{pfv} rice not.yet \textsc{q}\\
\enquote*{Have you eaten?}\\
\bigskip
氹 tam3 \enquote*{to coax}, \enquote*{to cheer s.o. up}\\
tam3*2\~{}tam3 (= tam3 jat1 tam3 氹一氹 coax:\textsc{delim})\\
\smallskip
tam3*2\~{}tam3 keoi5\\
coax:\textsc{delim} \textsc{3sg}\\
\enquote*{Coax him/her a bit.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
\textbf{\underline{Lexical (Nominal) Suprafix}}: {\footnotesize see also de Sousa (2024)}\\
a.k.a. 小稱變調 siu2 cing1 bin3 diu6 \enquote*{diminutive tone-change}\\
\smallskip
{\footnotesize However: Cantonese nominal suprafixes have been used so much that they often do not have a diminutive meaning \emph{(small / endearment meaning)}.}\\
\medskip
e.g.\\ 
糖 tong4 \enquote*{sugar}\\
糖 tong4*2 \enquote*{lolly, candy}\\ 
\medskip
comparable with Northern Mandarin -兒 -r, e.g.\\
頭 tóu \enquote*{head}\\
頭兒 tóur \enquote*{boss, chief}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
Cantonese lexical suprafixes:\\
Derivative morphology (word building)\\
\medskip
Usually applied to last syllable of a noun / noun stem, e.g.\\
{\footnotesize 蝴蝶 wu4 dip6*2 \enquote*{butterfly}\\
(cf. 蝴蝶灣 wu4 dip6 waan1 (butterfly bay))}\\
\medskip
Usually do not apply to other word classes, e.g.\\
{\footnotesize 鉗 kim4*2 \enquote*{pliers}, 鉗 kim4 \enquote*{to grab with pliers}}\\
{\footnotesize 碟 dip6*2 \enquote*{plate}, 碟 dip6 \textsc{clf}}\\
\medskip
Not quite predictable, e.g.\\
{\footnotesize 法蘭西 faat3 laan4*1 sai1 \enquote*{France}, 荷蘭 ho4 laan4*1 \enquote*{Holland},\\ \quad but 芬蘭 fan1 laan4 \enquote*{Finland}, 紐西蘭 nau2 sai1 laan4 \enquote*{New Zealand}\\
澳門 ou3 mun4*2 \enquote*{Macau},\\
\quad but 屯門 tyun4 mun4 \enquote*{Tuen Mun}, 江門 gong1 mun4 \enquote*{Kongmoon / Jiangmen}, 廈門 haa6 mun4 \enquote*{Amoy / Xiamen}}\\

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}

{\small 仔 zai2: another possible origin of the lexical suprafixes, but\\
\begin{itemize}
    \item lexical suprafixes and -仔 -zai2 already coexisted in the 19\textsuperscript{th} century Cantonese textbooks, with different meanings (仔 zai2 has a clear \textsc{dim} meaning)\\
    \item 仔 zai2 was in ordinary tone 2 and not suprafix tone 2*\\
    \item the 仔 zai2 theory explains tone 2* [˨˥] adequately, but tone 1* [˥] less well\\  
\end{itemize}
\smallskip
仔 zai2 less likely than 兒 ji2*1 as the origin of the Cantonese lexical suprafixes} (see de Sousa 2024)\\
{\footnotesize However, 仔 zai2 could have inspired further suprafixed forms, e.g. 麻仔 maa4 zai2 > maa4*2 \enquote*{measles} (Kwok 2016: 307)}
