\begin{frame}[fragile]{Tones}
\textbf{Tone values 調值} (phonetics) vs.\\ \quad \textbf{Tone categories 調類} (phonology)\\
\bigskip
\underline{Tone values 調值}: phonetic shape of tones 
\\People commonly use Chao's tone letters or tone numerals\\
\quad {\footnotesize (Chao Yuen Ren 趙元任)}\\
e.g. Standard Mandarin\\ 
官話 /kwan˥ xwa˥˩/ \textasciitilde{} /kwan\textsuperscript{55} xwa\textsuperscript{51}/\\

\underline{Tone categories 調類:}\\ tones in phonological system {\footnotesize (tonemes, allotones)}\\
{\footnotesize (names like)} tones A B C D, tones 1 2 3 4, etc.\\
e.g. Mandarin 旗 /tɕʰi2/ [tɕʰi\textsuperscript{25}], Cantonese 恥 /tsʰi2/ [tsʰi\textsuperscript{25}], Taiwanese 持 /tʃʰi5/ [tʃʰi\textsuperscript{25}]\\
{\scriptsize (with numerals, be careful whether people mean tone categories or tone values)}\\

\section{Suprafixes}

\subsection{Suprafixes 1}
\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
Suprafix: suprasegmental affix {\footnotesize (affix in the form of e.g. tone, stress)}\\
In the case of Cantonese: tonal affixes\\
\medskip
\textbf{suprafix ≠ tone-sandhi}\quad {\footnotesize although both are called 變調 in Chinese}\\
\bigskip
\underline{Tone-sandhi}:\\
\textsuperscript{(largely)} automatic \& predictable process of tones changing, \\
\quad given a particular phonological / morphosyntactic environ.\\
\medskip
e.g. Standard Mandarin tone 3 sandhi rule\\
買馬 mǎi mǎ > mái mǎ (= 埋馬 mái mǎ)\\
\smallskip
e.g. Shanghainese\\
三杯 se˥˧ pe˥˧ > se˥ pe˨˩\\
四杯 sz˧˦ pe˥˧ > sz˧ pe˦\\ 

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
Tone Sandhi in Cantonese?\\
\bigskip
e.g. Cheung 張 (1969)\\ 
different sandhi rules concerning ˥˧ > ˥ in Canton vs Hong Kong\\
\medskip
\emph{However, it is very hard to find people nowadays who contrast ˥˧ and ˥ consistently\\
\quad Hong Kong is nowadays basically all ˥}\\
\smallskip
Most Cantonese speakers do not have tone sandhi

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}

{\small \emph{(Suprafixes in Standard Cantonese are tone-substitutive)}}\\
Grammatical vs. lexical suprafixes:\\
\textbf{\underline{Grammatical suprafix}}:\\

\medskip
e.g. verbal:\\
\medskip
食 sik6 \enquote*{eat} > sik6*2 (= sik6 zo2 食咗  eat \textsc{pfv})\\
\smallskip
nei5 sik6*2 faan6 mei6 aa3?\\

\enquote*{Have you eaten yet?}\\
\bigskip
氹 tam3 \enquote*{to coax}, \enquote*{to cheer s.o. up}\\
tam3*2\~{}tam3 (= tam3 jat1 tam3 氹一氹 coax:\textsc{delim})\\
\smallskip
tam3*2\~{}tam3 keoi5\\
coax:\textsc{delim} \textsc{3sg}\\
\enquote*{Coax him/her a little bit.}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}

\smallskip
a.k.a. 小稱變調 siu2 cing1 bin3 diu6 \enquote*{diminutive tone-change}\\

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Suprafixes}
Cantonese lexical suprafix:\\
Derivative morphology (word building)\\
\medskip
Usually applied to last syllable of a noun / noun stem, e.g.\\
{\footnotesize 魚 jyu4*2 \enquote*{fish}, 蝴蝶 wu4 dip6*2 \enquote*{butterfly}\\
(cf. 魚生 jyu4 saang1 \enquote*{raw fish}, 蝴蝶灣 wu4 dip6 waan1 \enquote*{butterfly bay})}\\
\medskip
Usually do not apply to other word classes, e.g.\\
{\footnotesize 鉗 kim4*2 \enquote*{pliers}, 鉗 kim4 \enquote*{to grab with pliers}}\\
{\footnotesize 碟 dip6*2 \enquote*{plate}, 碟 dip6 \textsc{clf}}\\
\medskip
Not quite predictable, e.g.\\
{\footnotesize 法蘭西 faat3 laan4*1 sai1 \enquote*{France}, 荷蘭 ho4 laan4*1 \enquote*{Holland},\\ \quad but 芬蘭 fan1 laan4 \enquote*{Finland}, 紐西蘭 nau2 sai1 laan4 \enquote*{New Zealand}\\
\smallskip
澳門 ou3 mun4*2 \enquote*{Macau},\\
\quad but 屯門 tyun4 mun4 \enquote*{Tuen Mun}, 江門 gong1 mun4 \enquote*{Kongmoon / Jiangmen}, 廈門 haa6 mun4 \enquote*{Amoy / Xiamen}}\\

\end{frame}

仔 zai2 (\textsc{dim}): another possible origin of the lexical suprafixes. 
However:\\
\begin{itemize}
    \item 仔 zai2 was in ordinary tone 2 and not suprafix tone *2\\
    \item 仔 zai2 explains tone *2 [˨˥] reasonably, but tone *1 [˥] not well\\
    {\scriptsize e.g. mui6 + zai2 > mui6*2 is OK, but mui6*1 (?)}

\end{itemize}
