\section{Early Cantonese: phonology}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Early Cantonese}
Earliest text with elements identifiable as Cantonese:\\
《花箋記》faa1 zin1 gei3 \enquote{Flowery Scroll} (late Ming: 17\textsuperscript{th} C, or perhaps 16\textsuperscript{th} C)
{\scriptsize \textit{English translation thereof is said to have inspired Goethe's} „Chinesisch-deutsche Jahres- und Tageszeiten“}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{Early Cantonese}
Earliest Cantonese rime book:\\ 
《分韻撮要》fan1 wan5 cyut3 jiu3 (early Qing dynasty)\\
\smallskip
{\footnotesize 
Earliest known edition: 壁魚本《分韻撮要》\\eds. 武溪溫儀鳳岐山\& 姪武溪溫繼聖端石, undated (17xx ?); kept in BSB\\
\smallskip
Slightly later edition: 元亨本《分韻撮要》; kept in Leiden U\\
\smallskip
Most-famous dated edition:\\
《江湖尺牘分韻撮要合集》(1782)}\\
\medskip
Only recorded literary pronunciations.\\
\medskip
\textit{\small (Many older traits of Cantonese recorded in this book can still be found in many other Yue dialects)}

\end{frame}
